What it is

The professional credential for investment management, the work of research analysts and portfolio managers at funds, pensions, and other institutions: three sequential exams (Level I tools and ethics, II asset valuation, III portfolio management), roughly 300 hours of study each, plus 4,000 hours of work experience. Pass rates are typically below half at each level. It is a strong credential for careers in equity and credit research, portfolio and asset management, and institutional investing. For students headed to wealth management or financial planning, the CFP certificate is the better fit.

Timing

Undergraduates can sit for Level I in an exam window up to 23 months before their graduation month. For a Finance major the realistic window is senior year, once most of the courses below are done.

How the Finance plan maps

The CFA has no required coursework, so every course here is a recommendation. The major already covers MGT 340 (ethics), ECO 391 and FIN 300 (quantitative methods), FIN 300 and FIN 405 (corporate issuers), and FIN 410 (equity).

What Gatton offers

  • CFA Institute University Affiliation for the BBA in Finance and the MS in Finance: each program covers at least 70% of the CFA candidate body of knowledge.
  • Three undergraduate scholarships a year, each waiving the enrollment fee and cutting exam registration to $600, roughly half price. Students apply through the principal contact.
  • Real-money portfolio class (FIN 485): students manage part of Gatton’s Student Managed Investment Fund, now over $6 million, drawn from the University endowment, earn Bloomberg certification, and present to the endowment committee.
  • The CFA Institute Research Challenge, the Stock Pitch Competition, and the Finance Society.

Terms. FIN 400-level slot: one of the three upper-level finance courses the major requires. Choose-one: the major’s single choice among ACC 302, ACC 475, FIN 359, or another FIN 400-level course. Free elective: any course the student chooses that counts toward graduation hours.
