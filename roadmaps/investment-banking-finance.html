What it is

Investment bankers advise companies on raising capital and on mergers and acquisitions, and the same skills lead to private equity, corporate development, and capital markets roles. Hiring rests on technical skill in accounting, valuation, and financial modeling, and on a junior-summer internship that is usually recruited well before senior year. This sheet is a roadmap for a student who says they are interested.

Start early: the programs carry the first two years

  • Wall Street Scholars: a competitive cohort program for incoming freshmen and rising sophomores, with a one-credit class each semester of the first two years, Bloomberg Market Concepts, Wall Street Prep modeling seminars, alumni networking and city visits, and a finance or accounting internship before junior year. Apply through the Finance department’s Wall Street Scholars page.
  • Finance Society (student-run, open to join) and its Gatton Select program, a banking preparation curriculum for freshmen chosen by interview in the spring semester.
  • Wall Street Prep for students who want more financial modeling and technical interview preparation on their own.

Three things to know

1.
Start freshman year. Wall Street Scholars and Gatton Select both select first-year students, so the topic is worth raising with an interested student in the first year.
2.
Recruiting runs ahead of the courses. The courses fall in senior year, after the junior-summer internship is usually decided. Technical preparation before then comes from the programs and Wall Street Prep.
3.
It costs no extra hours, but the slots are shared. All four courses fill the three FIN 400-level slots and the choose-one. A student also pursuing the CFA or CFP competes for the same slots, and the extra courses move to free electives.

Terms. FIN 400-level slot: one of the three upper-level finance courses the major requires. Choose-one: the major’s single choice among ACC 302, ACC 475, FIN 359, or another FIN 400-level course. Free elective: any course the student chooses that counts toward graduation hours.
