Why this one

Same shape as GAP/C&O: a short first day, three solid days, an easy finish. The longest day is 72 miles, about Ohiopyle to Cumberland. The difference is the towns. We start with a night in Manhattan, sleep in Tarrytown, Poughkeepsie, Hudson and Troy, and finish in Saratoga with something to do the next day instead of a shrug at Mile 0.

What's new for us: getting out of Manhattan and the Bronx on day 1, one bridge into Albany on day 4, and real hills on days 2 and 3.

Day by day

Day 0

Get to the Battery

Travel
Drive to Saratoga Springs and leave the cars. Amtrak south to Penn Station with the bikes, about 3.5 to 4 hours. Ride 5 flat miles down the Hudson River Greenway to the hotel.
Sleep
Lower Manhattan. Marriott Downtown on West Street has bike storage and sits on the greenway. Backups: Club Quarters Wall Street, Holiday Inn FiDi, the Beekman. Bikes go in the room or in storage, never outside.
Eat
Big New York dinner. Nominations open.
Day 1

Battery to Tarrytown

about 30 mi
Ride
Harbor photo at the Battery seawall, then the official start at the Battery Park City Gateway kiosk. Greenway north the length of Manhattan, about 2 miles of Bronx streets to Van Cortlandt Park, then rail trail. One steep hill in upper Manhattan. Done by early afternoon.
Stop
9/11 Memorial pools, two blocks from the start. Little Island around mile 2.5. Little Red Lighthouse under the GW Bridge around mile 10. The granite marker where the trail leaves New York City.
Skip
The Intrepid, the Cloisters, anything that pulls us into the street grid.
Eat, drink
Captain Lawrence Brewing in Elmsford, on the trail about 3 miles before Tarrytown.
Sleep
Tarrytown, about 3 miles off the trail. We're in by early afternoon, so there's time for Sleepy Hollow Cemetery before dinner.
Maybe
Staten Island Ferry round trip before we roll: free, bikes ride the lower deck, about 25 minutes each way, past the Statue of Liberty twice. Skip it if we're running late.
Day 2

Tarrytown to Poughkeepsie

about 72 mi
Ride
The long one. North County and Putnam Trailways, a short road stretch at Brewster, then the Maybrook Trailway and Dutchess Rail Trail. Rollers all day, nearly all car-free.
Stop
The Appalachian Trail crossing near Pawling. The Great Swamp stretch just north of it. Hopewell Depot, a restored 1873 station on the trail, for the afternoon break.
Skip
Brewster beyond a water refill. Cold Spring and Beacon are good towns but down at the river, well off our line.
Eat, drink
Mill House Brewing in downtown Poughkeepsie. Or the Culinary Institute, 5 miles north in Hyde Park, by cab - student-run restaurants, possibly the best meal we've had on one of these, but only if they're open that week and we have a reservation.
Sleep
Downtown Poughkeepsie, close to the Walkway for the morning.
Day 3

Poughkeepsie to Hudson

about 61 mi
Ride
Walkway Over the Hudson first thing, 212 feet up, while it's empty. Rail trail to New Paltz and Rosendale, Kingston for lunch, then quiet roads past Tivoli, Bard and Olana, and over the Rip Van Winkle Bridge into Hudson.
Stop
Huguenot Street in New Paltz, stone houses from around 1700. The Rosendale trestle, 150 feet over Rondout Creek. The Stockade District's four corners in Kingston. The bakery in Tivoli.
Skip
FDR's home and the Vanderbilt Mansion. Half-day visits on the wrong side of the river.
Eat, drink
Lunch on Kingston's Rondout waterfront, or Keegan Ales. Clemson Brewing in New Paltz if the timing works. Dinner on Warren Street in Hudson; Hudson Brewing is near the waterfront.
Sleep
Hudson. It's a weekend-tourist town, so book early.
Day 4

Hudson to Troy

about 47 mi
Ride
Albany-Hudson Electric Trail through Kinderhook to Rensselaer, the Dunn Bridge into Albany, then the riverfront path north. The bridge is the one crossing that needs attention, and there's a short on-road gap around Watervliet.
Stop
Kinderhook's village green and the farmstand with ice cream. The Albany skyline from the river path.
Skip
Downtown Albany. See it from the water and keep rolling.
Eat, drink
Brown's Brewing on the river in Troy. Dinosaur Bar-B-Que as the backup. A real diner breakfast before day 5.
Sleep
Downtown Troy.
Day 5

Troy to Saratoga Springs

about 35 mi
Ride
Streets to Waterford, about 7 miles of Champlain Canal towpath, a few road miles on Route 4 into Mechanicville, then the paved Zim Smith Trail for 12 miles through Round Lake to Ballston Spa. Finish through Saratoga Spa State Park and Railroad Run into downtown. In by lunch.
Stop
The Waterford flight of locks, where the Erie and Champlain canals split. Round Lake's gingerbread cottages. A mineral spring in the state park - drink it once.
Eat, drink
Second breakfast at the Ugly Rooster in Mechanicville. Leah's Cakery in Round Lake. Saratoga for the finish dinner.
Sleep
Saratoga Springs.
Watch for
The Zim Smith has had closures with no notice online. Call Saratoga County Parks the week before; Route 50 is the way around.
Saturday

The backstretch, then home

Track
Races if the meet is on. If it isn't, horses still work in the morning at the Oklahoma training track across the street, and the viewing stand there is free.
Also
Saratoga battlefield by car, 20 minutes out. Riding it would have cost us the Zim Smith.

To lock down

  • Saratoga hotel. Book as soon as we have dates.
  • Amtrak bike spots. Roll-on racks, about $20 a bike, reservation required. Book them with the tickets.
  • Rental bike. For anyone not bringing one: a New York shop that delivers to the hotel, and how it gets back from Saratoga.
  • Hudson and Troy hotels. Once dates are set.
  • Manhattan greenway. Check for construction detours the month before.
  • Zim Smith Trail. Call the county the week before.
  • Culinary Institute. Call before we plan a dinner around it.
